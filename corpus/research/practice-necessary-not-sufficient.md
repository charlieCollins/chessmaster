# Deliberate practice is necessary but not sufficient

**Sources**
- Campitelli, G., & Gobet, F. (2011). Deliberate practice: Necessary but not sufficient.
  *Current Directions in Psychological Science, 20*(5), 280–285.
  LSE eprint: https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/102854
- Hambrick, D. Z., et al. (2014). Deliberate practice: Is that all it takes to become an
  expert? *Intelligence, 45*, 34–45 (meta-analytic reanalysis; deliberate practice
  explained about a third of the variance in chess skill).
- Burgoyne et al. (2019) — see [burgoyne-2019-domain-experience.md](burgoyne-2019-domain-experience.md).

## Findings
- Practice hours are strongly related to chess skill, and nobody reaches master level
  without thousands of hours of it.
- But players with similar practice hours end up at very different levels: practice
  explains a large share of the differences, not all of it (starting age and other
  individual factors matter too).

## Strength of evidence
Consistent across several datasets; all correlational.

## What it means here
- Build the habit of focused practice (the part the learner controls) and show
  progress honestly — accuracy and solve rate over time — rather than promising a
  rating curve.
