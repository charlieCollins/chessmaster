# Burgoyne et al. (2019) — domain-specific experience and chess skill

**Citation:** Burgoyne, A. P., Nye, C. D., Macnamara, B. N., Charness, N., & Hambrick, D. Z. (2019).
The impact of domain-specific experience on chess skill: Reanalysis of a key study.
*American Journal of Psychology, 132*(1), 27–38.
PDF: `corpus/raw/papers/burgoyne2019-domain-experience.pdf` (reference only).

## What they did
Reanalysed the data of Charness, Tuffiash, Krampe, Reingold & Vasyukova (2005) — a large
survey of rated tournament players' practice histories — with factor analysis and
structural equation modelling.

## What they found (quoted/paraphrased from the paper)
- Latent variables for **serious chess activity** and **formal instruction**, plus
  serious starting age, accounted for **63% of the variance in peak rating**.
- **Starting earlier helps**: serious starting age had a negative effect on peak rating
  (β = −.15) even after controlling for experience.
- The idea that formal instruction makes serious study *more effective* (Ericsson &
  Charness, 1994) was **not supported**.
- Conclusion: "serious study and other forms of domain-specific experience are important
  pieces of the expertise puzzle, but other factors must matter too."
- From the original 2005 study, as summarised in this paper: total hours of **serious
  study** predicted current and peak rating better than formal instruction or tournament
  play. For players **under 40**, several activities predicted current rating (serious
  study, tournament play, private instruction); for players **40 and older, only total
  hours of serious study** did.

## Strength of evidence
Correlational (self-reported practice histories), not an experiment — it shows what
strong players *did*, not that a particular regimen *causes* improvement. Still one of
the best datasets on chess practice.

## What it means here
- The Learn area should be a place for **serious solo study** (focused, effortful
  solving and review), not passive reading.
- For an adult learner this is the activity with the clearest link to rating; casual
  play alone is a weaker lever.
- Don't oversell: the app can't promise a rating gain per hour.
